Job to invoice

From Clock-In to Invoice on One Phone

The job is not done when the work is done. It is done when the time, the parts, and the invoice agree. Most owners close that loop at the kitchen table. The parts ticket is in the truck. The hours are a guess. The invoice goes out tomorrow, which becomes next week.

Job to invoice puts that loop on the phone you already have in your hand at the driveway. Clock in when you arrive. Log parts and receipts while the hood is open. Close the job when you leave. The invoice is drafted from what you logged. You approve it. Then it sends. A draft is not a bill until you say so.

That approval is the point. The system prepares. A person sends. Money does not move because a screen guessed.

How it works

  1. Clock in at the job.
  2. Log parts and receipts.
  3. Close the job.
  4. The invoice is drafted.
  5. You approve and send.

What you can change

Adjustable

The fields on the close-out, the way labor is described, and who is allowed to approve can change as the crew grows. A new part category should not require a new app.

Fixable

If a draft fails to save, the notes should still be on the phone and the failure should flag a person. A lost close-out is the bug we treat as urgent.

Flexible

Some crews want receipts photographed. Some want a short list of parts. The close-out bends around the way the tech already works in a driveway.

Customizable

Your job types, your labor lines, and the note the customer sees are written for your shop. Stripe can connect when you want a card payment on an invoice you have already approved.

When something goes wrong

If the phone is offline in a parking lot, the close-out stays on the device until it can sync. If a sync fails, you still have the draft and Thomas gets the alert. The customer is not billed from a half-saved job.

What it connects to

Google Workspace and Gmail can carry the invoice you approved. The phone app holds the clock and the parts. Cloudflare can host the sync. Stripe can connect for payment after approval. GitHub holds the app itself. n8n can notify you when a draft is waiting.

Fits at stage

This belongs at Grow, when jobs are leaving the driveway faster than invoices are leaving the kitchen. At Scale it is how more than one crew closes the same way.

Questions

Does the customer get a bill without me?

No. You approve, then it sends.

What if I forget a part?

Open the job and add it before you approve. The draft can be edited.

Can two techs log the same job?

The job has one record. People add lines to it instead of keeping private notes.

Follow one brake job. You arrive and clock in. The pad set and the rotor go on the record while you are still in the driveway, with a photo of the receipt if you want it. You close the job. A draft invoice is waiting before you start the truck. You look at it in the cab, fix a line, and approve. The customer gets the bill while the work is fresh, and you are not rebuilding the ticket from memory after dinner. That is the product. Adjust the lines when you change how you describe labor. Fix the sync when a phone cannot reach the server. Bend the photos and fields around dirty hands and cold weather. Keep the approval in a human hand so a wrong part never becomes a wrong bill on its own.

The comfort owners want is not a fantasy that nothing can be mistyped. It is a loop short enough that the mistake is caught before the customer sees it. Clock, log, close, draft, approve. Five words a new tech can learn on day one.

The driveway is a bad office and a good place to capture facts. The part number is on the box in your hand. The time started when you pulled in. Waiting until night means both facts get softer. The phone record keeps the hard version. You can add a note the customer said at the bumper. You can mark a job that needs a second visit. You can see yesterday's open drafts before the first call of the morning. When a second tech joins, they close their own lines into the same record, so the invoice is one story instead of two texts you have to merge.
