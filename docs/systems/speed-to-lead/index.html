Lead follow-up

Speed-to-Lead: Automatic Follow-Up for Every New Lead

A lead is a person who raised a hand. They filled a form, tapped an ad, or asked for a quote. Then they wait. If nobody calls, the hand goes down and the person calls a competitor who did answer. Speed-to-lead is the path that runs after that hand goes up, whether you are free or not.

The order is deliberate. A call comes first, because a voice still settles more jobs than a silent inbox. If nobody answers, a text goes out. If the text sits, an email follows. You receive the lead and a record of what was said, so you are not reconstructing the conversation from memory at night.

This is follow-up, not a promise that every lead becomes a job. Some people were curious. Some were comparing. The system makes sure the curious ones hear from you while they still remember filling the form.

How it works

  1. A lead fills a form or an ad.
  2. The system calls them.
  3. No answer, it texts.
  4. Still nothing, it emails.
  5. You get the lead and what was said.

What you can change

Adjustable

The words in the text, the delay before the email, and which leads count as worth a call are rules you can edit. A winter slowdown and a summer rush do not have to share one script.

Fixable

If the call step fails, the text still goes. If the text fails, the email still goes. Each break raises a flag instead of vanishing.

Flexible

A form on your site, a lead from an ad, or a name you type in can enter the same path. The source can differ. The follow-up shape stays one you recognize.

Customizable

Your offer, your tone, and the questions you want answered are written for your trade. A mover and a coach should not sound like the same company.

When something goes wrong

The fallback is the next channel. Call, then text, then email. You still get the lead even when the customer never picks up. Thomas gets an alert when a step itself breaks.

What it connects to

Forms land through the site on Cloudflare. Gmail carries the email step. Twilio carries call and text. n8n runs the order. Google Workspace holds the record you read later.

Fits at stage

Start here if you are the only one answering. Keep it when a crew is busy on jobs, because the lead should not wait for the crew to drive back.

Questions

Does it text people who did not ask?

It follows up the person who asked. Consent for later texts is a separate checkbox on our own forms, and it starts unchecked.

Will I see the conversation?

Yes. You get the lead and what was said.

Can I turn a channel off?

Yes. If you want calls only, or texts only after a certain hour, that is a rule change.

Walk a lead named only by the form they filled. They asked for a haul on a Wednesday. The system calls. They are in a meeting, so the text arrives with a simple question and your number. They reply after lunch. You see the reply and the original request together, not as a mystery thread. If they never reply, the email still gives them a way back, and you are not left wondering whether anyone tried. That sequence is the product. Adjust the wording when your offer changes. Fix the path when a carrier rejects a number. Bend it around the tools you already pay for. Keep it specific to the jobs you want, so a tire question and a full move do not get the same speech.

Owners lose leads in the gap between "we will call them back" and the moment someone is free. The gap is where the other company wins. Speed-to-lead closes the gap with a fixed order you can explain to a new hire in one minute: call, text, email, then show me the record. No one on the crew has to remember the order. The order is the system.
