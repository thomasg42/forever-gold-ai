AI operations

AI Operations: One System Instead of Ten Tabs

A growing shop collects tools the way a truck collects sockets. One tab for the calendar, one for mail, one for the leads, one for the invoices, one for the notes, and a group text that contradicts all of them. AI operations is the work of making those tools one system, with an assistant that knows your business and a screen where the numbers sit together.

It starts with a map. How does a lead become a job, a job become an invoice, an invoice become a paid week? Where do people retype the same name? Where does a failure go unnoticed? Then the tools you already use get connected. Then an assistant is built on your answers, not on a generic company. Then the numbers you care about land on one screen. Then every failure raises an alert.

This is the Scale system. It assumes the front door, the follow-up, and the crew tools exist or are being built. It is how they stop being separate projects.

How it works

  1. We map how your business runs.
  2. Connect your tools.
  3. Build an assistant that knows your business.
  4. Your numbers on one screen.
  5. Every failure raises an alert.

What you can change

Adjustable

The questions the assistant may answer, the numbers on the screen, and the alerts you want can change when the business changes. A dashboard that cannot be edited becomes another tab you ignore.

Fixable

The standard is that a failure raises a flag. A workflow that dies quietly is the defect. Thomas gets the alert, and the path can be repaired without you discovering it from a customer.

Flexible

Your stack is allowed to be the stack you have. The map starts from Google Workspace, the calendar, the mail, the phone, and the site. It does not begin by throwing those out.

Customizable

The assistant knows your services, your rules, and your words. A screen for a one-crew shop and a screen for two locations should not be copies of each other.

When something goes wrong

Alerts are the design, not an apology. If a step fails, a person hears about it. If the assistant is unsure, it does not invent a fact. The unsure item comes to you, the same way an unsure phone call does.

What it connects to

Google Workspace, Google Calendar, Gmail, Twilio, ElevenLabs, n8n, Cloudflare, and GitHub are the tools this system is built to join. The join is the work. A new tool gets added when it earns a place on the map, not because it is fashionable.

Fits at stage

Scale. You have crews or locations, and the cost of ten tabs is now higher than the cost of one map. Start and Grow build the pieces. This is where the pieces become an operation.

Questions

Do I have to move all my tools?

No. We connect what you use. A move happens only when a tool is in the way.

Will the assistant guess?

It answers from what you approved. Unsure items come to a person.

What numbers show up?

The ones you choose to watch. A number we have not confirmed does not get printed as a fact.

An owner with two crews described the week as "I open mail, then the calendar, then the texts, then I forget which lead I already called." The map for that shop was simple: a lead has one record, a job has one calendar block, a close-out has one draft, and a failure sends one alert. The assistant on top of that map can answer "what is open tomorrow" from the calendar, not from a guess. The screen shows the leads waiting, the jobs booked, and the drafts that still need approval. That is the product. Adjust the screen when a number stops mattering. Fix the alert when it fires too often or not at all. Bend the connections around the tools you will not give up. Keep the assistant on your facts so it sounds like your operation and not like a brochure.

Fewer mistakes, in this sense, is not a claim that the week will be perfect. It is a design: type a fact once, check before you act, flag the failure, and leave a person where money or a promise is involved. The map makes that design visible. The assistant and the screen keep it in daily use.
