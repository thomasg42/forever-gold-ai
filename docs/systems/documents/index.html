Documents

Paperwork and E-Signatures Without the Pile

Paperwork piles up in the seat, the office, and the camera roll. Agreements get signed on a hood with a wet pen. Two people leave with different copies. A month later nobody can find the version that was signed.

Document systems do two jobs. First, a pile can become a draft: drop in the paperwork, let AI prepare the record, and have a person approve it before it is treated as final. Second, an agreement can be sent, signed on a phone, and stored so both sides keep the same signed PDF with an audit trail.

The person in the middle is not optional. Drafts stay drafts until someone who knows the job says they are right. That is how errors leave the process without anyone pretending the software is perfect.

How it works

  1. Drop in the paperwork.
  2. AI drafts the record.
  3. A person approves.
  4. Send the agreement.
  5. It is signed on a phone and both sides get the same signed PDF with an audit trail.

What you can change

Adjustable

The template, the fields a signer fills, and the order of who signs can change when the agreement changes. You should not be stuck with last year's form.

Fixable

If a signature link fails or a file does not save, the alert goes to a person. The customer is not told the document is finished when it is not.

Flexible

A one-page service agreement and a longer packet can use the same idea: draft, approve, send, sign, keep one PDF. The length can differ.

Customizable

Your clauses, your name, and your logo belong on the document. The trail of who signed and when belongs to that document, not to a generic folder.

When something goes wrong

If a signer stops halfway, the document stays unfinished and you can see that. If email delivery fails, you still have the record and a way to send the link again. Nothing is marked finished until the required people have signed.

What it connects to

Google Workspace and Gmail can deliver the link and the finished PDF. Cloudflare and GitHub carry the app. The signed file is the thing both sides keep, so you are not hunting through a camera roll.

Fits at stage

Documents show up at Scale, when the pile is no longer something one owner can hold in their head. Crews and locations make a shared trail matter.

Questions

Who approves the draft?

A person. The software prepares. It does not declare the record final.

Do both sides get the same file?

Yes. The signed PDF and the audit trail go to both.

Can I change a form later?

Yes. A new version can go out. Old signed copies stay as they were signed.

Imagine the packet that rides in the truck all week. Intake sheets, a service agreement, a photo of a permit. Instead of retyping them on Sunday, you drop them in. A draft comes back in your language, with the blanks still blank where the page was unreadable. You correct two lines and approve. When a customer needs to sign, they do it on their phone, and you both keep the PDF. The trail says who signed and when. That is the product. Adjust the template when your terms change. Fix the delivery when a link expires. Bend the fields around the way your crew already fills out paper. Keep a human approval on anything that states a fact about the job.

Owners fear paperwork because it hides mistakes until a dispute. A short loop, with a person at the approval, pulls the mistake forward to a moment when it is cheap to fix.

A signed agreement is a memory both people can open. The audit trail is the list of what happened to that file: who opened the link, who signed, and when the PDF was stored. If a customer calls three months later and says the terms were different, you open the PDF you both kept, not a photo of a clipboard. New hires can send the current form without asking you to find it. Old forms stay attached to the jobs that used them, so a change next month does not rewrite last month. The drop-in pile is separate from the signature path. A messy intake photo becomes a draft you correct. A signature request starts from a form you already approved. Mixing those two jobs is how a half-read scan becomes a contract. They stay distinct on purpose.
