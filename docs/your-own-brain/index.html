Your own brain

Your business, in one brain.

Every invoice, receipt, photo, note and customer record lands in one place, from your phone and every computer you work on. It grows with you. The more business you do, the more it knows.

Where is it right now?

Today your business lives in five places.

  • Receipts ride in the truck, the glovebox and a jacket pocket.
  • Invoices sit in one inbox. Quotes sit in another.
  • Job photos live on your phone, and on your crew’s phones.
  • Notes are on a pad, in your texts, and in your head.
  • The one person who knows where everything is, is you.

That works until somebody asks for it.

The day someone asks

Three days every owner has.

Tax season

Your accountant asks for every receipt from last year. You either hand over one folder, or you lose a weekend to the truck, the inbox and the shoebox.

A customer pushes back

A customer says the job was never quoted that way. You pull up the quote, the photos and the signed agreement, or you argue from memory.

A new hire starts

The new hire asks how you do it. The answer is already written down, or you explain the business again for the fifth time.

Why start now

The pile only gets bigger.

Tax records are not a one-year problem. Every month the business runs on memory adds more paper to dig through later. Every month it runs on one brain, each receipt is filed the day it comes in.

The filing date does not move. The receipts are either ready when it comes, or they are not.

3 years

is how long the IRS says to keep most business tax records. Employment tax records: at least 4 years.

IRS, How long should I keep records?

How it works

Capture it. It lands in one place. Ask for it.

  1. Capture it where you are. Snap the receipt at the counter, save the invoice on the office computer, forward the email. Nobody changes how they work.
  2. It lands in one place. Your phone, your laptop and the office computer all feed the same brain, filed the same way every time: by customer, by job and by month.
  3. Ask for it. When tax season comes, or you need that one photo, you know where it is. Your own AI can find it for you and answer from it.

What goes in it

Everything only you know about.

Receipts and invoices

Snapped at the counter or saved from email, filed by job, customer and month.

Customers

Who they are, what they asked for, what you quoted and what they paid.

Agreements and paperwork

Signed agreements, permits and forms, next to the job they belong to.

Job photos and notes

Before and after pictures and the notes from the job, where the next person can find them.

How you do things

Your prices, steps and checklists, written once so nobody has to ask you twice.

The numbers

What came in and what went out, from the same records, ready when someone asks.

It grows with you

The more business you get, the more you can build.

On day one it holds this year’s receipts. A year in, it holds every customer, every job, every quote and every lesson the business learned.

Everything else Thomas builds stands on it. A receptionist answers from it. Follow-up knows who the customer is. The numbers come from the same records you hand your accountant. A new location or a new crew is added once, and the brain knows.
